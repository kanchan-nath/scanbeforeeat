Deterministic Classification

The Scientific NOVA Framework

Formulated by epidemiologist Professor Carlos Monteiro and his public health team at the University of São Paulo, endorsed by the UN Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and the World Health Organization. NOVA classifies foods not by chemical macronutrients, but by the physical, biological, and chemical processes used in their manufacture.

Standard: FAO Guidelines (2019)
NOVA 1 eco

Unprocessed & Minimally Processed

Fresh fruits, vegetables, eggs, raw meat, legumes, whole grains

Edible parts of plants or animals with zero industrial processing, or processed strictly by drying, crushing, filtering, roasting, boiling, or non-alcoholic fermentation without adding salt, sugar, or fats.

Cellular Matrix: Intact Recommended
NOVA 2 oil_barrel

Processed Culinary Ingredients

Oils, butter, lard, sugar, salt

Substances obtained directly from Group 1 foods or from nature by processes like pressing, refining, grinding, or mining. Used in domestic and restaurant kitchens to season and cook Group 1 foods.

Form: Extracted Use In Moderation
NOVA 3 lunch_dining

Processed Foods

Canned vegetables with salt, artisanal cheese, freshly baked bread, cured fish

Simple food products manufactured essentially by adding salt, sugar, oil, or other Group 2 substances to Group 1 foods to enhance durability or sensory qualities. Most contain 2 to 3 ingredients.

Ingredients: 2-3 items Acceptable
NOVA 4 warning

Ultra-Processed Foods (UPF)

Instant noodles, carbonated soft drinks, mass snacks, reconstituted meat

Industrial formulations manufactured mostly or entirely from substances derived from foods and additives, with little or no intact Group 1 food. Characterized by high palatability and synthetic emulsifiers.

Bio-Impact: Disruptive Restrict / Avoid
Clinical Impact & Bio-Informatics

Why Food Matrix Disruption Matters

Ultra-processed foods dismantle the natural physical food matrix—accelerating digestion rates, blunting normal satiety hormones (leptin and GLP-1), disrupting gut microbiome diversity, and elevating systemic micro-inflammation.

Dietary Prevalence
57%

of total daily caloric intake in standard US adult diets derives directly from Ultra-Processed Foods according to national NHANES cohort bio-surveillance datasets.

Dataset: CDC / NHANES Epidemiological Survey

Grocery Aisle Heuristics

Scientific Red Flags Checklist

When inspecting an ingredients panel without a barcode scanner handy, apply these laboratory-backed heuristics to identify cosmetic food modifications and hyper-palatability formulations.

lightbulb The Golden Rule

“If an 11-year-old child cannot recreate it with ingredients found in a 19th-century kitchen garden, it is almost certainly NOVA 4.”

— Clinical Nutrition Consensus Guideline
warning

Industrial Pantry Disconnect

Contains 5+ ingredients you cannot pronounce or find in a standard kitchen pantry (e.g. isolate powders, invert hydrolysates).

warning

Synthetic Simple Carbohydrates

Includes high-fructose corn syrup, dextrose, invert sugar, or maltodextrin designed for rapid glycemic spiking.

warning

Cosmetic Azo Food Colorants

Has artificial food colorants (e.g. Tartrazine E102, Sunset Yellow E110, Allura Red E129) added strictly to disguise processing loss.

warning

Industrial Emulsifiers & Thickeners

Contains industrial emulsifiers, thickeners, or stabilizers (Polysorbate 80, Carboxymethylcellulose, Soy Lecithin) that thin gut mucus lining.

warning

Abnormal Shelf Life Resilience

Shelf life exceeds 12 to 24 months without requiring refrigeration, preserved via chemical sequestrants and BHT/TBHQ.
